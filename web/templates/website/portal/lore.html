{# I · THE DIVIDED REALM #}
I

The Divided Realm

For longer than any living chronicle can measure, the known realm has been divided between two great powers.

To the west lie the lands of the Valroian: old kingdoms, fortified valleys, high citadels, sacred groves, merchant roads, monasteries, and ancestral holds bound together by uneasy vows of mutual defense. Among their chief domains are the ancient kingdom of Aldemar, the fortress-valley of Ironvale, the sacred woodlands of Elyndor, the merchant city-state of Highcrest, and the great mountain citadel of Thornhold.

To the east stretches the dominion of Mordrath: a harsher realm of black fortresses, iron principalities, volcanic highlands, ancient ruins, war camps, hidden temples, and territories ruled by strength, fear, oath, and ambition. Its greatest powers include the ruling seat of Zod's Reach, the volcanic forges of Korrath, the iron-walled city of Dreadmarch, the shadow-haunted Ravenspire, and the deep ruins of Vorgoth, whose builders were forgotten before the Sundering.

Between them lies no clean border.

The center of the realm is a broad scar of disputed territory known collectively as the Contested Marches. Within this vast frontier lie the ruined province of Caldern, the ever-shifting Grey Fords, the blighted Ashen Corridor, the fortress-town of Broken Gate, and the lawless trade-hub of Wyrm's Hollow — a place where Valroian coin and Mordrath iron change hands in the same hour.

The realm is bounded by natural barriers that have shaped centuries of strategy. To the north, the Frostveil Mountains form an impassable wall of ice and stone. The southern coasts are dominated by the Sea of Shattered Glass — named for the treacherous reefs that have claimed more invasion fleets than any navy. Between the faction territories runs the great river known as the Severed Spine, whose bridges and fords have been contested in every major campaign of the war. Fortresses change hands. Villages swear new allegiance to survive. Old roads disappear beneath siegeworks. Mercenaries become lords. Heroes become traitors. Traitors become kings.

Neither faction has ever permanently controlled the Marches. Both believe they eventually must.

{# II · THE WAR OF PASSAGE #}
II

The War of Passage

The people of the realm call the struggle by many names. The oldest surviving chronicles call it:

“

The War of Passage. It is not a single war. It is centuries of wars, truces, betrayals, crusades, invasions, dynastic feuds, holy campaigns, rebellions and uneasy peace — all pieces of one conflict that has never truly ended.

The Sundering of Crowns

According to the oldest surviving accounts, the realm was not always divided. There are fragments of chronicles describing an earlier age when western and eastern kingdoms traded, intermarried and fought smaller wars without viewing one another as enemies by nature.

That age ended during an event remembered as the Sundering of Crowns.

No single account agrees on exactly what happened.

Valroian historians claim eastern warlords attempted to seize relics said to grant authority over the ancient kingdoms.

Mordrath chroniclers insist western rulers betrayed a sacred accord and used the crisis to justify conquest.

Religious texts claim something far worse occurred: that powers older than kings interfered directly in mortal affairs.

Whatever the truth, several royal lines collapsed almost simultaneously. The Throne of Aldemar was abandoned mid-session. The Warden-Kings of Elyndor sealed their inner sanctums and vanished. The eastern Forge-Lords of Korrath turned on one another in a single bloody night. Armies crossed borders. Temples burned. Old alliances shattered. In the chaos, a warlord calling himself Zod — whether a name, a title, or an idea — bound the surviving eastern powers under a single iron will.

From the chaos emerged two competing visions of civilization. The western kingdoms increasingly bound themselves through mutual defense, law, oath and shared institutions. The eastern powers embraced sovereignty through strength, personal allegiance, conquest and the belief that authority belongs to those capable of keeping it.

Those philosophies eventually hardened into the two great factions known today: Valroian and Mordrath.

The War of Passage has continued ever since. Its chapters are written in the names the chroniclers gave them: the Siege of the Seven Standards, the Burning of the Grey March, the Winter of False Truces, the Red Harvest Campaign, the Sack of Thornhold's Outer Ward, the Night of the Shattered Crown, the Accord of Ashes (which lasted eleven years before both sides accused the other of violating its terms), and a hundred lesser conflicts whose names have been forgotten by all but the scholars who catalogue them.

{# III · THE VALROIAN WEST #}
III

The Valroian West

The Valroian are not a single kingdom. They are a vast alliance of crowns, free cities, ancestral houses, temples, military orders and independent peoples who have agreed that survival requires cooperation.

OATH Freely given, never broken
DUTY To protect those beneath the banner
LEGACY What we leave behind defines us

To a Valroian, an oath freely given has weight. A ruler is expected to protect those beneath their banner. A warrior earns honor not merely by victory but by what they choose to defend.

This does not make the West peaceful. Valroian nobles feud. Merchant houses sabotage rivals. Priests argue doctrine. Ancient races remember insults mortals have forgotten. Generals compete for glory. Kings distrust other kings.

The alliance survives because its members believe Mordrath domination would be worse than their disagreements with one another.

Western Realms & Strongholds

Western fortifications are traditionally built to endure. The capital-citadel of Thornhold anchors the northern passes. Ironvale controls the valley roads that feed half the alliance's armies. The walled city of Highcrest serves as the West's primary mercantile hub, its counting-houses and guild halls as influential as any throne room. Aldemar remains the ceremonial heart of the old kingdom — its courts host the alliance councils, its archives preserve the oldest western chronicles. The sacred woodlands of Elyndor are less a kingdom than a trust: ancient forest realms guarded by the Wardens, dotted with standing stones, glades consecrated to forgotten pacts, and ruins whose age predates human settlement in the region. The monastic fortress of Whitehold in the southern highlands serves as the Sanctum of the White Flame's primary archive and hospital.

Valroian warfare emphasizes organized defense, disciplined formations, protected supply lines, trained orders and the gradual destruction of an enemy's ability to continue fighting.

Yet the West has produced conquerors of its own. Its chronicles are filled with rulers who spoke of defense while marching east beneath banners of liberation. To the people of Mordrath, Valroian honor often looks remarkably similar to conquest once an army crosses the Marches.

{# IV · THE MORDRATH EAST #}
IV

The Mordrath East

Mordrath is less an alliance than a hierarchy of powers. Its eastern territories are dominated by great houses, warlords, cults, military brotherhoods, arcane orders and ancient bloodlines that recognize strength as the ultimate foundation of political authority.

POWER Authority belongs to the capable
WILL The furnace that forges leaders
ASCENDANCY Rise. Or be forgotten.

A Mordrath ruler who cannot hold power does not deserve it. A commander who cannot inspire fear or loyalty will soon serve someone who can. A promise has value because breaking it has consequences.

Mordrath society can appear chaotic to outsiders, yet beneath the brutality lies an intricate network of debts, blood ties, patronage, challenges, vendettas and sworn loyalties. Eastern rulers frequently fight one another. Those conflicts do not necessarily weaken Mordrath. They are viewed by many eastern traditions as the furnace through which worthy leaders are discovered.

Eastern Dominions & Strongholds

Mordrath fortifications favor intimidation as much as defense. Zod's Reach is the political center of the eastern dominion — a vast fortress-palace carved into the shoulder of a mountain, its throne hall known as the Hall of Reckoning. From here House Zod commands the obedience of every power that wishes to remain relevant. Korrath is the industrial heart of the East: volcanic forges, smelting pits, and armories that have supplied eastern armies for generations, controlled by a council of forge-barons who answer to Zod. The iron-walled city of Dreadmarch guards the primary eastern approach to the Marches — its garrison is the largest permanent military encampment in the known realm. Ravenspire is a tower-city built atop older, nameless ruins; its libraries and spires house the Ashen Covenant's most dangerous archives. The deep ruins of Vorgoth are older than any surviving civilization — even Mordrath warlords send only expendable expeditions into its depths.

The Mordrath do not generally regard themselves as evil. They believe the western kingdoms hide weakness behind laws, councils and inherited privilege. To them, Valroian mercy preserves the incapable. Valroian peace protects corrupt rulers. Valroian law allows lesser people to command greater ones.

Mordrath philosophy offers a harsher promise: rise high enough, and no birthright can deny you. Fail, and the realm owes you nothing.

{# V · THE CONTESTED MARCHES #}
V

The Contested Marches

Between West and East lies the region most players will hear spoken of with fear, ambition and opportunity: the Contested Marches.

The Marches are not one province. They are the shifting central territories where Valroian and Mordrath influence collide. No treaty has ever permanently settled them.

Some communities have changed allegiance so many times that their oldest buildings contain both western heraldry and eastern battle marks. Ruined watchtowers stand beside rebuilt keeps. Old battlefields become farmland. Farm fields become battlefields again. Abandoned roads lead toward fortresses whose names have been erased from surviving maps.

Who Walks the Marches

Soldiers Assigned to frontier duty by both factions
Adventurers Seeking fortune, fame, or answers
Mercenaries Paid by whoever holds the coin
Spies Information is worth more than gold
Merchants Dangerous routes. Greater profits.
Scholars Studying ruins older than the war
Refugees Displaced by centuries of conflict
Criminals Where laws end, outlaws begin
Priests Serving faiths that predate the factions
Aspiring Heroes …and future tyrants

For ordinary people, the Marches are dangerous. For ambitious people, they are irresistible.

{# VI · THE VALROIAN SECTS #}
VI

The Valroian Sects

The Valroian alliance contains many lesser orders and houses, but four institutions command influence across much of the western realm.

{# SECT 1: Dawnshield #}
☼

The Order of the Dawnshield

Primary Military Defenders · Fortress Order

The Dawnshield is the most publicly recognized military order of the Valroian alliance. Its members traditionally serve as defenders of western settlements, roads, fortifications and civilians during times of war.

Their symbol is a rising sun behind a battered shield. Their doctrine teaches that victory is meaningless if nothing remains worth protecting afterward.

Dawnshield knights and soldiers are known for discipline, defensive warfare, fortress fighting and refusing to surrender positions others would abandon. Their current Lord-Commander, Marshal Aldric Veyne, is said to have held the Gate of Sorrows for seventeen days against a Mordrath siege with fewer than two hundred defenders — and refused to retreat even after reinforcements arrived, claiming the walls themselves had earned the right to keep the ground. Their critics accuse them of being rigid, self-righteous and too willing to sacrifice soldiers in defense of symbolic ground. Their supporters call them the wall behind which civilization survives.

“ When the last gate breaks, the Dawnshield becomes the gate.
{# SECT 2: Silver Hart #}
🦌

The Silver Hart Compact

Diplomacy · Intelligence · Political Unity

The Silver Hart Compact is a coalition of nobles, scouts, diplomats, merchants and frontier houses whose influence reaches far beyond the western courts. Where the Dawnshield represents open military strength, the Silver Hart survives through relationships.

Its agents negotiate treaties, secure supply routes, settle disputes, organize intelligence networks and quietly prevent western rivalries from becoming civil wars. The current Compact-First is Lady Seraphine Calder, a diplomat so subtle that three separate wars are believed to have ended without either side realizing she had brokered the peace. The emblem of the Compact is a silver stag beneath a crown of winter branches.

Some members are honorable diplomats. Others are spies who have spent entire lives maintaining false identities. The Compact believes unity is the West's greatest weapon. Its enemies claim unity achieved through secrets is merely another form of control. Among its most shadowed agents is Kael Morrow, who has spent twenty-three years posing as a Mordrath grain merchant named Torvin Ash — and who has reportedly spoken with House Zod's inner circle on three separate occasions without being discovered.

“ A sword may win the field. A promise decides who owns it tomorrow.
{# SECT 3: Wardens of Elyndor #}
❄

The Wardens of Elyndor

Wilderness · Ancient Sites · Forgotten Threats

The Wardens of Elyndor are guardians of ancient western lands, wilderness routes, sacred places and ruins predating the current factional age. They include rangers, wardens, druids, scholars, hunters and warriors whose loyalties frequently lie with the land itself as much as any crown.

The name Elyndor comes from an old western word whose exact meaning has been lost. It is variously translated as The Living Realm, The First Green, or That Which Remembers. Their symbol is a pale tree surrounded by seven stars.

The Wardens are especially suspicious of reckless magic, uncontrolled expansion, cursed ruins and anyone who disturbs places deliberately sealed by earlier civilizations. Their First Warden, an ancient dryad known only as Orenth, is believed to have walked the western forests for over three hundred years — and speaks of the Sundering as though it happened last season. They often know paths absent from royal maps. They also possess records hinting that the War of Passage may be younger than the threat buried beneath it.

“ Kings remember generations. The earth remembers everything.
{# SECT 4: White Flame #}
☲

The Sanctum of the White Flame

Faith · Healing · Knowledge · Archives

The White Flame is one of the most powerful religious and scholarly institutions in the Valroian West. Its temples maintain archives, hospitals, sanctuaries, schools and reliquaries. The White Flame itself is symbolic rather than literal in most traditions — it represents truth that survives corruption.

Members believe darkness is not defeated through ignorance but through understanding. Their priests therefore preserve forbidden histories that other western institutions would rather destroy. The current High Luminary is Archivist Elowen Thrace, a scholar who personally transcribed seventeen banned Mordrath texts into secure Valroian vaults — and who argues publicly that the West's refusal to study enemy philosophy is its greatest strategic weakness. The Sanctum's healers are respected throughout the alliance. Its inquisitors are feared almost as much as Mordrath assassins. The chief Inquisitor, Varen Ashford, has never lost a subject who had information he wanted — a fact that disturbs his colleagues nearly as much as it impresses them.

The order's greatest controversy lies in its belief that knowledge itself cannot be evil — only its use. This has led White Flame scholars to study artifacts, sorcery and texts that many western rulers consider dangerous.

“ The flame does not fear the darkness. It reveals what waits inside it.
{# VII · THE MORDRATH SECTS #}
VII

The Mordrath Sects

Mordrath contains countless houses, warbands and cults, but four organizations possess exceptional influence throughout the eastern dominion.

{# SECT 1: House of Zod — DOMINANT #}
☤

House of Zod

Dominant Mordrath Power · Political · Military PRIMARY SECT

House of Zod is the primary and most powerful Mordrath sect. It is among the oldest surviving eastern powers and currently stands as the dominant political and military force within Mordrath society. The current ruling authority is High Lord Maelkor Zod, the ninth to bear the name in unbroken succession — though whether that succession represents bloodline, appointment, or something stranger is a question House Zod treats as a capital offense to ask publicly.

Its origins are surrounded by deliberate contradiction. Some histories describe the first Zod as a warlord who united seven armies. Others call Zod a dynasty. Some forbidden eastern texts suggest Zod was originally a title rather than a name. House Zod rarely clarifies the matter.

Its symbol is traditionally a dark crown suspended above a crimson blade. The House believes power must constantly prove its legitimacy. Weak rulers invite rebellion. Weak armies invite invasion. Weak civilizations invite extinction.

Unlike many eastern warbands, House Zod values organization intensely. Its officers maintain disciplined military structures, intelligence networks, taxation systems, fortified territories and carefully recorded obligations. This is one reason the House has survived while hundreds of louder conquerors disappeared.

Its greatest leaders understand a lesson many Mordrath warlords never learn: fear can seize a throne. Administration keeps it. House Zod serves as the principal force capable of compelling rival Mordrath powers to act together against Valroian campaigns. When Zod calls the eastern banners, even ancient enemies listen. Not always willingly.

“ Power is not inherited. It is inherited only until someone stronger arrives.
{# SECT 2: Ashen Covenant #}
♻

The Ashen Covenant

Arcane · Transformation · Forbidden Knowledge

The Ashen Covenant is an arcane and religious order obsessed with transformation through destruction. Its members believe fire exposes truth. Metal becomes stronger in flame. Forests become fertile after burning. Civilizations, they argue, obey the same law.

The Covenant includes sorcerers, warlocks, alchemists, priests and scholars willing to experiment with forces rejected by western institutions. Their current leader is Pyremaster Sycorax, a warlock of such advanced age that even House Zod's genealogists cannot determine when she was born — some records suggest she predates the Sundering itself, though the Covenant dismisses this as superstition. Their emblem is a black hand holding a coal that never cools. They maintain hidden libraries containing texts stolen, purchased or recovered from ruins across the realm. The Covenant's most dangerous keeper is Archivist-Major Vorn, an illithid whose memory spans centuries and whose catalog of forbidden spells is said to fill a chamber whose door has not been opened in forty years.

House Zod tolerates the Covenant because its knowledge is invaluable. House Zod also watches it constantly. There are persistent rumors that senior members of the Ashen Covenant believe Mordrath itself is merely another structure that must eventually burn.

“ Nothing reveals what it truly is until the fire reaches it.
{# SECT 3: Black Thorn Synod #}
✿

The Black Thorn Synod

Eastern Law · Oaths · Judgment · Legitimacy

The Black Thorn Synod is a network of priests, judges, interrogators and political advisers who maintain many of the eastern traditions governing oath, punishment and legitimacy. Their current High Justiciar, Magister Severa Korr, has personally overseen the oath-binding of twelve Mordrath warlords and the oath-breaking executions of four more — one of whom was her own nephew. Her presence at a negotiation is said to be worth an extra legion. Their emblem is a black thorn wrapped around a crimson circle.

Despite Mordrath's reputation for lawlessness, eastern political life depends heavily upon recognized custom. Challenges must sometimes follow formal rules. Debts may transfer through bloodlines. Certain sanctuaries cannot be violated without consequence. Some oaths remain binding even between enemies.

The Synod remembers these laws. It also interprets them. This gives it enormous power. A warlord may command ten thousand soldiers and still hesitate before being declared oathless by the Synod. An oathless ruler may retain an army. They rarely retain allies.

Critics claim the Black Thorn manipulates ancient custom to control leaders stronger than itself. The Synod's response is simple: strength without order is merely an animal with a weapon.

“ Every crown rests upon an oath, whether its wearer admits it or not.
{# SECT 4: Iron Veil #}
🛠

The Iron Veil

Espionage · Assassination · Counterintelligence

The Iron Veil is the least understood of the great Mordrath sects. Officially, it is a brotherhood devoted to protecting eastern rulers from espionage, assassination and internal betrayal. Unofficially, it is Mordrath's most feared intelligence network.

The Veil recruits scouts, killers, informants, infiltrators, poisoners, couriers and individuals whose names never appear in official records. Its current leadership is unknown. Some believe a figure called the Masked directs the Veil's operations from a location that shifts with the moon. Others insist the Masked does not exist — that the Veil is a collective hallucination maintained by operatives who each believe someone else is in charge. Its emblem is a blank iron mask divided by a vertical red line. The organization does not maintain a public hierarchy. Members often know only a handful of other operatives. One of the few confirmed operatives whose name is known outside the Veil is Shrike — though whether Shrike is a single person, a rotating identity, or a legend deliberately cultivated is a question not even House Zod's intelligence apparatus can answer with certainty.

Rumors claim the Veil has agents inside Valroian courts, merchant houses, military orders and even western temples. More disturbing rumors claim it maintains equal numbers inside Mordrath itself. House Zod employs the Veil. Whether House Zod controls it is another question entirely.

“ If you saw the knife, it was not the Veil.
{# VIII · FAITH, POWER & ALLEGIANCE #}
VIII

Faith, Power & Allegiance

Religion throughout the realm is complicated. Neither Valroian nor Mordrath represents a single faith. Different races, regions and cultures maintain their own gods, ancestors, spirits, philosophies and sacred traditions.

Faction allegiance is political and cultural rather than a simple division between holy and unholy.

Valroian temples may disagree violently with one another.

Mordrath priests may serve deities of law, war, death, knowledge or conquest.

Some gods are worshipped on both sides under different names.

Others appear to have chosen sides.

A few ancient traditions claim the gods themselves are divided because of something that happened before mortal history began. No surviving scripture proves the claim. No surviving scripture completely disproves it either.

This ambiguity is deliberate. The true nature of the divine in Rites of Passage remains one of the deepest mysteries of the realm — and one that players may help uncover.

{# IX · LIFE IN A DIVIDED WORLD #}
IX

Life in a Divided World

Most people are not kings, knights or warlords.

They are farmers trying to harvest before armies arrive.

Blacksmiths repairing tools during peace and weapons during war.

Merchants calculating whether a dangerous road is worth the profit.

Innkeepers listening to soldiers boast about battles they barely survived.

Parents hoping their children inherit a trade instead of a feud.

Refugees carrying the keys to homes that no longer exist.

Scholars preserving books whose authors belonged to forgotten kingdoms.

Adventurers Are Different

They travel where sensible people do not. They enter ruins sealed for generations. They accept work from dangerous patrons. They discover objects governments would kill to possess. They cross borders. They uncover old truths. And sometimes they become powerful enough that factions must notice them.

“

This is where the player enters the history of Rites of Passage. Not as the chosen ruler of the world. Not as a guaranteed hero. But as someone with the opportunity to become important.

Reputation must be earned. Enemies will remember. Allies will have expectations. Power creates choices. Choices create consequences. Legends begin long before anyone realizes they are watching one.

{# X · THE AGE AHEAD #}
X

The Age Ahead

The current age is unstable.

Neither Valroian nor Mordrath has achieved decisive victory. The Contested Marches remain divided. Ancient ruins are being uncovered. Old bloodlines are resurfacing. Forgotten relics have begun appearing in markets and battlefields.

Scholars argue over prophecies previously dismissed as allegory. Rulers on both sides prepare for wars they publicly insist they do not want.

In the West Western orders quietly reinforce the frontier. The Silver Hart sends agents east. The Wardens warn that something beneath the oldest lands has begun to stir.
In the East House Zod strengthens its grip upon the eastern powers. The Ashen Covenant searches for knowledge buried before the Sundering. The Iron Veil sends agents everywhere.

And ordinary people continue living beneath banners that may change before the next harvest.

Whatever comes next will not be written by historians alone.

It will be written by those willing to enter the realm.
