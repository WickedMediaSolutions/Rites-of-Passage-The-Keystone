Development Update September 7, 2026

The Realm Nears Readiness

Rites of Passage has reached a major development milestone.

After weeks of concentrated development, most of the core game foundation is now in place. The server-side architecture, character systems, combat engine, skills and spells framework, inventory and equipment pipeline, mob definitions and AI scaffolding, quest infrastructure, economy and shop systems, faction and PvP rules, guild and sect structures, social and communication systems, regeneration and progression mechanics, and the public website/player portal have all been built, tested, and integrated.

What this means is that Rites of Passage is increasingly becoming a cohesive game rather than simply a collection of independent systems. Individual components that were once tested in isolation now interact: a character created through the portal's character-generation wizard can explore, fight creatures using combat mechanics that consult equipped gear and trained skills, earn currency, purchase from shops, accept and complete quests, join a guild, align with a sect, and participate in faction-based PvP in contested territory. The foundation supports all of these activities working together as a unified whole.

This does not mean the entire playable world has been populated room-by-room. The realm itself — the cities, forests, fortresses, contested marches, and hidden places of the Valroian West and the Mordrath East — has yet to be fully brought to life. But the systems required to support that world are now substantially in place.

The focus of development is increasingly moving away from building foundational systems and toward populating the realm, creating rooms and zones, designing monsters and encounters, crafting items and equipment content, worldbuilding, balancing, testing, documentation, polish, and final integration work.

Development progress across key areas includes:

Character Systems
Twenty-four races, ten professions, character creation wizard, base and derived statistics, progression framework, HP/Mana/Stamina resources, and regeneration mechanics.
Combat
Physical attack resolution, hit/miss calculation, weapon damage, armor mitigation, damage-type system, and combat-state management.
Skills & Spells
Data-driven skill and spell registry with structured resource costs (mana or stamina), targeting, damage types, healing, and skill-use validation.
Equipment & Inventory
Fifteen equipment slots (head to off-hand), weapon and armor definitions, consumable items, quest items, and full equip/unequip pipeline.
Factions & PvP
Faction-based alignment determined by race selection (Valroian / Mordrath), area-level PvP modes (Safe, Contested, Arena, Free-for-All), PvP eligibility rules, and war-point tracking.
World Infrastructure
Mob definitions and factory, mob AI scaffolding, mob spawning and respawning, loot tables, quest definitions and progress tracking, shop catalogues, currency economy, guild and sect registries, and social/emote systems.
Player Portal
Account registration and authentication, Dashboard, character creation wizard, Armory foundations, public documentation (Getting Started, Game Guide, Lore, Command Reference), and account management.
Testing & Documentation
Extensive automated testing across twenty development phases covering character foundation through final integration audit.

Each of these systems has been built with the same philosophy: Rites of Passage is not simply a traditional MUD recreated unchanged. It is a modernized persistent text RPG that preserves the depth, command-driven gameplay, exploration, and character development that define the genre, while providing a foundation that can support a substantial world.

Development is now entering the stage where the realm itself can increasingly become the focus. The tools are ready. The foundation is solid. The world is waiting to be built.

Nexus Development September 7, 2026

Nexus Client Enters Production

Development has begun on Nexus, the planned native Windows client for Rites of Passage. Nexus is intended to become the dedicated player client for connecting to and experiencing Rites of Passage, providing a purpose-built experience rather than relying exclusively on generic MUD clients or browser-based access.

The intended workflow and feel of Nexus is inspired heavily by the classic MegaMud client used for MajorMUD, while being designed specifically around Rites of Passage and its Evennia server foundation. Where MegaMud provided a structured interface for navigation, combat, inventory, and character management within a text-based world, Nexus aims to deliver a similarly focused and integrated experience tailored to the unique systems and identity of Rites of Passage.

Nexus development is in its earliest stages. Production has started, and further updates will be provided as the client progresses toward its initial release. No release date has been set.

World Lore

A Realm Divided

The established identity of the Rites of Passage world has come into sharp focus. The western realm stands under Valroian influence — old kingdoms, fortified valleys, high citadels, sacred groves, merchant roads, monasteries, and ancestral holds bound together by uneasy vows of mutual defense. The eastern realm answers to Mordrath — a harsher dominion of black fortresses, iron principalities, volcanic highlands, ancient ruins, and territories ruled by strength, fear, oath, and ambition. Between them lies no clean border: the broad scar of the Contested Marches, where fortresses change hands, villages swear new allegiance to survive, and both powers believe they eventually must prevail.

The philosophical divide between the two powers is now woven into the identity of the world itself. The Valroian way is defined by Oath, Duty, and Legacy. The Mordrath path is forged in Power, Will, and Ascendancy. Within Mordrath, the dominant sect is the House of Zod, wielding influence across the eastern territories. The faction conflict is no longer merely background lore — it is being integrated into the core fabric of gameplay, determining faction alignment at character creation (by race), governing PvP eligibility, and shaping the allegiance structures of guilds and sects.

Website

The Rites of Passage Portal Takes Form

The public-facing Rites of Passage website and player portal has undergone extensive development. What began as a simple homepage has been transformed into a comprehensive portal built around the visual language of near-black charcoal, cold grey fortress stone, blackened iron, weathered steel, and restrained antique gold — a Gothic medieval fantasy presentation worthy of the game it represents.

The portal now provides account registration and authentication, a player Dashboard, a browser-based play option via the web client, and a growing suite of public information pages: Getting Started for new players, the Game Guide as a complete field manual, the Lore & World codex, a public Command Reference, Races and Professions compendiums, the Downloads area, and the foundations of the Armory for character profiles. The portal is intended to become the bridge between the website and the game itself rather than simply a promotional homepage — every page serves a function for players who will inhabit this world.

World Building

Tools for a Larger Realm

Development attention has turned to the infrastructure required to build and manage a substantial game world. Purpose-built world-editing tools are being developed to provide the framework for constructing the realm — rooms, zones, connections, and the spatial logic that will define exploration, travel, and territory control across the Valroian West, the Mordrath East, and the Contested Marches between them.

Robust world-building infrastructure is essential because the intended scope of the realm is ambitious. The world is not designed to be a handful of connected rooms with a thin veneer of description. It is planned as a layered environment where cities contain districts, districts contain streets, streets contain important locations, and each location can hold encounters, merchants, quest-givers, and points of factional interest. The tooling to construct that depth efficiently and consistently is in active development.

As the realm moves from foundation toward population, these internal world-building capabilities will be the instrument through which the world takes physical form — ready for players to experience through the Nexus player client, the browser-based web client, or traditional MUD clients.

29 Aug 2026
World Systems

Building a World That Can Fight Back

Development expanded beyond player-facing systems to encompass the broader world infrastructure that makes a multiplayer RPG feel alive. Mob and NPC definitions were established alongside a factory system for creating live creature instances from static data. Mob AI scaffolding provides the foundation for combat behaviour, while mob spawning and respawning systems ensure that zones can be populated with appropriate threats.

Quest definitions and progress tracking were implemented — supporting kill objectives and collect objectives with XP and item rewards — creating the structural framework that quest content will populate. Shop catalogues and a full currency economy (copper through platinum) were built, allowing items to be bought and sold with fund validation and stock tracking. Guild and sect registries, alongside social and emote systems, provide the scaffolding for player organization and interaction.

It is important to distinguish between systems and content. The systems described here are the engines, rules, and pipelines — the infrastructure that makes gameplay possible. The content — the specific monsters that inhabit a given forest, the quests offered by a particular NPC, the items sold by a named merchant — is the population work that follows. The systems are largely complete. The population is the next horizon.

25 Aug 2026
Game Systems

Steel, Spells and Survival

The focus of development shifted heavily toward the gameplay systems that make characters more than database records. The combat engine was built with physical attack resolution, hit/miss calculation based on configurable chance thresholds, weapon damage scaling by type, and armor mitigation granting flat damage reduction per equipped piece. A fifteen-slot equipment system (from head and chest down to main-hand and off-hand) was implemented alongside inventory management for weapons, armor, consumables, quest items, and miscellaneous goods.

The skills and spells framework was one of the most substantial undertakings of this period. A data-driven registry of over two hundred skills and spells was established, each with defined resource costs (mana or stamina), targeting requirements, damage types spanning physical, elemental, and special categories (piercing, slashing, fire, water, earth, air, poison, disease, holy, unholy, psychic, and bleed), healing capabilities, and full use-validation pipelines. Skills can unlock through profession progression, and their use is governed by resource availability, targeting rules, and combat-state management.

This was the point where individual systems began interacting to form actual gameplay. A character's equipped weapon now feeds into combat damage calculation. Their armor class reduces incoming damage. Their available mana or stamina gates which skills and spells they can activate. Their profession determines which abilities are available to unlock. The pieces of a real RPG were clicking into place.

{# ── AUGUST 21, 2026 ──────────────────────────────────────────── #}
21 Aug 2026
Characters

Characters Begin to Take Shape

Development focus moved heavily into the systems that define player characters. The character creation pipeline was built, supporting twenty-four distinct races split evenly between the Valroian and Mordrath factions. Race selection determines a character's faction alignment — a foundational design decision that means players do not independently choose Valroian or Mordrath. Their race is their allegiance.

Ten professions were defined, each with different strengths, stat distributions, and skill-unlock paths. The character statistics system was implemented with five core stats — Strength, Intelligence, Wisdom, Dexterity, and Constitution — each influenced by race and profession choices. A progression framework ties level advancement to experience gain, with base stats, HP, mana, and stamina all scaling as characters grow in power. Character state management (standing, resting, meditating, combat, and dead) governs regeneration rates and combat eligibility.

The character creation wizard on the portal was integrated with this system, allowing new players to select a race, choose a profession, and step into the world with a fully configured character built on the same data structures that drive gameplay server-side.

{# ── AUGUST 17, 2026 ──────────────────────────────────────────── #}
17 Aug 2026
Development

A New Foundation for Rites of Passage

Development begins on the current generation of Rites of Passage, built on Evennia as its server foundation. Evennia provides a modern Python-based MUD/MUSH/MUX development framework that handles networking, database persistence, session management, and the command dispatch architecture — allowing development to focus on game design rather than server infrastructure.

The goal from the outset has not been simply to recreate a traditional MUD unchanged. Rites of Passage aims to build a modernized persistent text RPG that preserves the depth, command-driven gameplay, exploration, and character development that define the genre, while pushing beyond the limitations of older codebases. The early focus is deliberately on architecture and foundational systems rather than filling the world immediately. A game of this scope needs a solid skeleton before it can wear its flesh.

The project structure is established with clear separation between the game's world data layer (pure Python, testable without a running server), the typeclass layer (Evennia's object model), the command layer (player input handling), and the web/portal layer (Django templates and views). This clean architecture will prove essential as the system grows in complexity over the weeks ahead.
